# Arcane Orb: lore bible (draft 1)

Tone: a warm storybook with a wink. Corny jokes are allowed, sincerity wins
in the big moments. The world is not one aesthetic: glowing gardens, golden
meadows, old ruins, sky castles, singing caves and a giant machine.

---

## Part 1: The world in one page

**The Weavers.** Long ago, the Weavers spun the world's magic into a single
sphere of living light: the Orb. Then they built something to keep it safe.

**The Lattice.** The Lattice is that something. It is a great machine made of
bricks, and its only order is one line long: *"Keep the light safe."* The
Weavers forgot to add "until when."

**The vanishing.** One day the Weavers were gone. The Lattice kept working. It
decided the safest place for light is behind a wall, so it began walling it
up: brick by brick, realm by realm, until the sky went dark. It is not evil.
It is the world's most overprotective babysitter, and it has never once taken
a break.

**The Orb chooses.** The Orb cannot be held, only guided, so the people it
chooses carry a Deflector: a paddle of hard light that turns where it flies.
Wherever the Orb strikes, the Lattice breaks. The Orb did not choose one hero.
It chose seven very different people, the **Keepers**, and it keeps trying
them one at a time. (That is why every run is a new attempt.)

**The realms.** The Lattice has three layers, like an onion nobody asked for:

- **The Outer Realms**: Neon Grove, Sunset Meadow, the Ancient Ruins and the
  Cloud Kingdom. Held by the **Prism Colossus**.
- **The Deep Realms**: the Machine Works, where every brick is cast, and the
  roots below. Held by the **Crucible Golem**.
- **The High Realms**: the Crystal Caverns and the peaks above them. Held by
  the **Crystal Warden**, the heart of the Lattice.

**The Keystones.** The three bosses are the Keystones: the bricks that hold
the whole Lattice up. Break all three and the walls come down.

**The catch.** The Lattice always grows back. Somewhere a single brick shifts,
and the work begins again. The Keepers keep coming back too.

## Part 2: The overarching story

The prologue-to-epilogue cards tell one run. Across many runs, the game tells
a bigger story in three layers, each revealed a little at a time.

**Layer 1: the run (already in the game).** Break the three Keystones. Light
returns. The Lattice regrows. Try again.

**Layer 2: the mystery (revealed through origin lore pages).** Each Keeper
holds one piece of the question *"Where did the Weavers go?"*

- The Arcanist finds Weaver writing that ends mid-sentence.
- The Engineer finds that Lattice bricks are warm, as if something inside
  them is alive.
- The Guardian guards a temple door with the Orb's shape carved on it.
- The Crystal Warden's song is not a battle cry. It is a lullaby.

**Layer 3: the truth (the true ending).** The Weavers did not vanish. They
wove themselves into the Orb, to become the light they loved. The Lattice
was built to protect them, and that is why it grows wherever the Orb goes.
The bricks are not chasing the Keepers. They are chasing their makers.

The Crystal Warden was the first thing the Weavers ever made, and it has been
singing them to sleep for three hundred years. In the true ending the Keepers
do not break it. They let the Orb answer: the Weavers' light speaks to the
Warden, and for the first time someone tells the Lattice, *"You can stop now.
You did a good job."* The bricks settle, and become roads, houses and
bridges instead of walls.

Then a single brick shifts. It just wanted to say goodbye.

## Part 3: The Keepers

Names are proposals. The in-game origin names (Guardian, Berserker and so on)
stay as they are; the names would show up in lore pages, quips and art.

### Guardian: Dame Oriel

Oriel has guarded the same temple door for twenty years. Nobody has come
through it. Nobody has tried to. She has polished it 7,300 times. When the
Orb rolled up the temple steps and bumped into her shins, she took it as a
promotion.

She fights by standing her ground: catch the Orb dead centre and her shield
answers with a wave of holy light. Her Aegis is the wall she always wanted to
be. She is the group's big sister, keeps a list of everyone's bedtimes, and
is the only one who reads the Weavers' rules. She suspects the door she
guarded matters more than anyone knows. (It does.)

*Relations:* thinks the Berserker is reckless (correct), thinks the Gambler
is a bad influence (also correct), trusts the Engineer with her armour.

### Berserker: Brakka

Brakka is a mountain clan builder who woke up from a nap to find a Lattice
wall built through the middle of her house. It has been personal ever since.
She does not want to save the world exactly. She wants to hit every brick in
it, and saving the world is a nice side effect.

Her Rage builds with every brick she breaks and fades the moment she slows
down, so she never slows down. Bloodlust is simply what Brakka is like before
breakfast. Under the noise she is soft-hearted: she rebuilds every house she
knocks through on the way.

*Relations:* sparring partner and secret admirer of the Guardian's patience;
the Pyromancer's favourite person to set things on fire next to.

### Arcanist: Professor Vell

Vell is the last scholar of the Weavers, which mostly means he owns the most
books about them. He could never pick a favourite element, so the Orb picks
for him: fire, frost, storm and void, every eight seconds, like a wizard
shuffling a playlist.

He is the one asking the real question: where did the Weavers go? He has
found their writing on Lattice bricks, and every message ends mid-sentence.
He reads them aloud to the group at camp. Nobody has asked him to.

*Relations:* argues theory with the Engineer every night and loses about
half the time; tutors the Pyromancer, who does not listen.

### Engineer: Rivet

Rivet grew up in the Machine Works, the giant engine that casts every Lattice
brick. She knows bricks the way bakers know bread, and she noticed what
nobody else did: Lattice bricks are warm. Something inside them is alive.

So Rivet does not want to smash the Lattice. She wants to fix it. Her paddle
is a homemade laser rig, and it hits hardest when she holds still and thinks,
which is her whole personality. Overload is what happens when she stops
thinking.

*Relations:* the Stormcaller's opposite in every way (one holds still, one
never stops moving), and they are best friends because of it. Keeps the
Guardian's shield shiny.

### Gambler: Lucky Sol

Sol was in the middle of a card game on a Cloud Kingdom casino barge when the
Orb fell from the sky and landed on the table. He looked at it, looked at his
cards, and declared it part of his winning hand. Nobody has been able to
argue him out of it.

Sol is powered by gold. The more he carries, the harder he hits, so the
question every run is spend or hoard. He insists his luck is a skill. His
Jackpot insists otherwise. He owes everyone in the group money, including the
Orb.

*Relations:* the Guardian's long-suffering project; runs a betting pool on
every boss fight, and the Berserker always wins.

### Pyromancer: Cinder

Cinder was an apprentice in the foundry towns under the Crucible, where the
Golem's fires burn day and night. She loves fire more than anything, and the
Crucible was the biggest, warmest fire in the world. She is the Keeper who has
to put it out.

She fights with patience disguised as chaos: every fifth hit sparks, and
every hit on a burning brick stokes it hotter. She will tell you she is in it
for the explosions. She is mostly in it so that the foundry towns can see
the sun.

*Relations:* the Arcanist's worst student and favourite one; the Berserker's
partner in crime; secretly the Golem's biggest fan.

### Stormcaller: Zephyr

Zephyr is a skywatcher from the Cloud Kingdom, which used to have the best
view of the stars in all the realms. Then the Lattice roofed it over. Zephyr
has not stood still since: static builds when you keep moving, and Zephyr
treats sitting down as a personal defeat.

When the charge is full, the Orb turns into a storm, and for a few seconds
Zephyr finally looks happy. Thunderstrike always targets the biggest, most
stubborn brick in the room, which Zephyr says is a coincidence.

*Relations:* the Engineer's best friend and opposite; competes with Cinder
over whose element looks cooler.

## Part 4: The Keystones (bosses)

All three are loyal Lattice constructs following their one order. None of them
are villains; they are workers doing their jobs far too well.

- **The Prism Colossus** splits light into a thousand pieces and hides each
  in a different brick, so no thief could ever take it all. When it breaks,
  the Outer Realms see the stars for one night.
- **The Crucible Golem** runs the forge that casts every brick, and has not
  taken a holiday in three hundred years. It hums while it works. It is
  slightly offended you are here.
- **The Crystal Warden** was the first thing the Weavers ever made, and
  their last mistake. It is the heart of the Lattice, and it sings. It is
  singing a lullaby to someone who is not there.

## Part 5: Changes to the existing story cards (story.go)

The story stays the same. Only the clashing names change:

| Now | Proposed | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "You are the last **Warden** of the Orb." | "The Orb has chosen you, a **Keeper**." | "Warden" is also the final boss; there are seven heroes, not one last one. |
| Prologue title "The Last Warden" | "The Orb Chooses" | Same clash. |
| "Three **Guardians** hold the Lattice together." | "Three **Keystones** hold the Lattice together." | Guardian is also an origin. |
| "Their constructs did not." | "The Lattice did not, and it had one order: keep the light safe." | Sets up why it is walling the world. |
| Epilogue, after the brick shifts | add: "And somewhere, the Orb is already choosing." | Ties regrowth to the next run. |

## Decisions for you (❓)

1. **Names**: keep, swap or drop the Keeper names (Oriel, Brakka, Vell, Rivet,
   Sol, Cinder, Zephyr).
2. **True ending**: how to unlock it. My suggestion: win once with every
   origin, then the next win plays the true epilogue. This is a later build
   step (new cards and text), not part of this one.
3. **Twist level**: the "Weavers are in the Orb" reveal. Keep it, or keep
   the mystery unsolved.
